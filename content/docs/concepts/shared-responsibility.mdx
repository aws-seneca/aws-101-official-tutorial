Security and compliance are shared between AWS and you.

- **Security of the cloud** is AWS's job: the hardware, software, networking and facilities that run AWS services.
- **Security in the cloud** is yours. How much that covers depends on the service.

| Service type | Example | AWS handles | You handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure | EC2 | Hardware, network, facilities | The guest OS and its patches, your app, the security group |
| Platform | RDS | Also the OS and the database engine | Database settings, users, the data |
| Abstracted | S3, DynamoDB | Everything below your data | Who can access the data, and protecting it |

## In the lessons

You were responsible for: running `dnf update` on the instance, opening only the ports the app needs, and choosing a database password. AWS patched the PostgreSQL engine for you because RDS is a platform service.

You are always responsible for your account: IAM users, MFA, and who has access. See [IAM](/docs/concepts/iam).
