- A **Region** is a geographic area where AWS clusters data centres, such as `ca-central-1` in Canada or `us-east-1` in Virginia. Each Region has at least three Availability Zones.
- An **Availability Zone (AZ)** is one or more data centres with independent power, cooling and physical security. Zones in a Region are physically separate but joined by redundant, very low-latency networks.

## Why it matters

- **Most resources live in one Region.** If you create something in Canada (Central) and then look at the console set to N. Virginia, you will not see it. This is the most common "where did my instance go?" in the lessons.
- **One zone can fail.** Spread copies across two or more AZs and the app survives. That is what Multi-AZ RDS and an Auto Scaling group across zones do. See [High availability](/docs/architecture/4-high-availability).
- **Pick a Region near your users**, and one that has the services you need. Prices also differ by Region.

## In the lessons

Both lessons use Canada (Central), `ca-central-1`, and ask you to check the region selector at the top right of the console before you create anything.
